Generate Control groups: Pick 10 random prompts. Generate them across 3 seeds. Add these to your dataset to act as a "baseline" for what is inherently likable.

Run a simple Chi-Square test on the most common nouns and adjectives to get a quick "kill-list" of words you should never use again.

Use a Large Language Model (LLM) as a synthesizer: Feed your top 20 "Like" prompts and bottom 20 "Dislike" prompts into an LLM (like GPT-4) and ask: "Act as a prompt engineer. Given these positive and negative examples, write a 5-point style guide explaining the structural, grammatical, and lexical differences between these two sets." LLMs are remarkably good at spotting syntactic patterns (e.g., "You use commas after adjectives in the bad set, but use spaces in the good set").

The ultimate test: Take the top 5 "good" words you discover and the top 5 "bad" words. Create 10 new prompts that are identical except you swap those words. Generate them. If the "good word" versions win 8 out of 10 times, you have successfully solved your problem.

Environment and spatial layering
Describe foreground, middle ground, background, and atmosphere.
State relationships rather than listing objects.

Example:
Broad sheets of wet black ink spread across pale stone in the foreground; 
the woman occupies a circular platform in the middle ground; 
temple columns dissolve into mist behind the animal.

Example:

shadows and a broad pearlescent highlight across the animal's white fur;
